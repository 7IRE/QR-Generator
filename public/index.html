What is error correction?

A QR code stores redundant data so it can still be scanned even if part of it is damaged, obscured, or dirty — like a QR on a worn label or with a logo overlaid in the centre.

Low (7%) — smallest code, least resilient. Best for clean digital screens.
Medium (15%) — good balance for most uses.
Quartile (25%) — ideal for print or branded QRs with overlaid logos.
High (30%) — maximum resilience, largest code.

FAQ

Is QR Studio free to use?

Yes — completely free, with no account, no limits, and no watermark. The source code and Wasm build pipeline are open source on GitHub.

Is my data stored or uploaded anywhere?

No. QR codes are generated entirely in your browser via WebAssembly. Nothing is logged, stored, or sent to a server or any third party.

Can I add a logo to my QR code?

Yes. Use the logo overlay option to place an image in the centre. Pick a higher error-correction level (Quartile or High) so the code still scans reliably with a logo on it.

What is error correction?

Redundant data that lets a QR code keep working when part of it is damaged, obscured, or dirty. Levels range from Low (7%) for clean digital screens to High (30%) for print and branded codes with logos.

Which formats can I download?

PNG for everyday use and SVG for crisp, scalable graphics. You can also copy the code to your clipboard or share a link.
